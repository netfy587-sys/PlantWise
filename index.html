Choosing a Site, Soil and Containers

Three things decide most outcomes: light, soil and drainage. Get these right before choosing seeds.

Sunlight

Fruiting crops such as tomato, chilli and brinjal need 6 to 8 hours of direct sun. Leafy greens and herbs like coriander, mint and spinach manage with 4 to 5 hours. Track the sun on your site for one day before planting.

Soil

Good soil is loose, crumbly and rich in organic matter. A simple pot mix is one part garden soil, one part compost and one part coco peat or sand for drainage. Soil pH between 6.0 and 7.0 suits most vegetables.

Containers

Herbs and greens need pots about 15 to 20 cm deep. Tomato, chilli and brinjal need 30 cm or more. Every container must have drainage holes, since standing water rots roots.

Raised beds

Beds 20 to 30 cm high warm faster, drain better and reduce bending. Keep them about 1.2 m wide so you can reach the middle from both sides.

Easy Herbs and Vegetables to Start With

Make A Choice Today by picking two or three crops that match your light and space rather than trying everything at once.

Tomato

Needs full sun, support stakes and steady watering. Uneven watering causes cracked fruit.

Chilli

Thrives in warm weather and pots. Heavy fruiting for months from one plant.

Spinach

A cool-season leafy green that grows in 4 to 6 weeks. Cut outer leaves to keep it producing.

Mint

Very vigorous. Grow it in its own pot, since the roots spread and crowd other plants.

Sowing, Planting and a Seasonal Calendar

Timing depends on climate. In much of northern India there are three main seasons for growing: winter (Oct to Feb), summer (Feb to June) and monsoon (June to Sept). Adjust to your local weather.

How to sow seeds

  1. Fill pots or trays with moist, loose mix and level the surface.
  2. Sow at a depth about two to three times the seed's width; very fine seed is only pressed onto the surface.
  3. Cover lightly, mist gently and keep the mix moist but not soaked.
  4. Thin seedlings to the spacing on the packet so plants do not compete.
  5. Move seedlings outdoors gradually over a week (hardening off) before final planting.
Tip: Fresh seed germinates better. Check the packing date and store leftover seed in a cool, dry place in a sealed container.

Watering, Feeding and Composting

Watering

Water deeply when the top 2 to 3 cm is dry, ideally in the early morning. Aim at the base, not the leaves. Shallow daily sprinkling creates weak roots. Mulch with dry leaves or straw to cut evaporation.

Feeding

Leafy crops like nitrogen; fruiting crops need more phosphorus and potassium once they flower. Compost, vermicompost or well-rotted manure every three to four weeks is usually enough for beginners.

Composting

Mix "greens" (kitchen scraps, grass) with "browns" (dry leaves, paper). Keep it damp like a wrung-out sponge and turn it every week or two. Avoid meat, dairy and oily food.

Explore. Choose. Enjoy. The best growing method is the one you will keep up. A short daily look at the plants often prevents bigger problems later.

When to harvest

Harvest leafy greens and herbs often and while young, since regular cutting encourages new growth. Pick tomatoes and chillies when fully coloured, and cucumbers and okra when small and tender. Morning, after the dew dries, is a good time for harvesting. Wash everything before eating and, if you use any spray, follow the waiting period on its label.

Frequently Asked Questions About Growing Food and PlantWise

How much sun do vegetables need?

Fruiting vegetables such as tomato, chilli and brinjal need 6 to 8 hours of direct sun. Leafy greens and many herbs manage with 4 to 5 hours.

What is the easiest vegetable to grow at home?

Radish, spinach, lettuce, coriander, mint and green onion are quick and forgiving, especially in small containers.

How deep should a pot be for vegetables?

Herbs and greens need about 15 to 20 cm. Tomato, chilli and brinjal do better in pots 30 cm or deeper with drainage holes.

How often should I water a vegetable garden?

Water deeply when the top 2 to 3 cm of soil is dry. Containers in hot weather may need water daily, while garden beds usually need it two or three times a week.

Can I grow vegetables without a garden?

Yes. Pots, grow bags, hanging planters and window boxes all work, provided the spot receives enough light and the containers drain well.

Key Points From the PlantWise Guide

  • Light, soil and drainage decide most results; check them before buying seeds.
  • Start with two or three easy crops that fit your sun and pot size.
  • Sow in the right season for your climate and thin seedlings to proper spacing.
  • Water deeply and less often, feed with compost, and mulch to hold moisture.
  • Look at your plants daily; early action solves most pest and disease problems. See What Everyone Loves about home growing: fresh food a few steps from the kitchen.
